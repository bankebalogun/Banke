# Content Strategy: Opera/Ballet History x Pop Culture x Tech

**Content rule:** nothing in the automated bank centers on death, murder,
or other controversial/violent subject matter. This ruled out an entire
early pillar idea ("Villains You Root For" — Sweeney Todd, Bluebeard, the
Lucia mad scene) along with a few individual scripts (the Phantom's 1896
chandelier fatality, the Orpheus myth, castrati history, Bizet's death).
Those are gone from `data/topics.json`; the pillar list below reflects
what's actually left in rotation, not the original brainstorm.

Context: two weeks in, four topics posted — Sweeney Todd, Phantom of the
Opera, Coppelia (+ modern AI parallel), Carmen (+ hip-hop opera crossover).
The throughline already working across all four: **"the classical/historical
thing you dismissed is secretly the ancestor of something you already love."**
That's a strong, repeatable format. The gap isn't the idea — it's that you've
only been mining one angle (origin story) instead of the several angles
buried in the same source material.

## The 10 content pillars (this is where the gaps are)

Each of your 4 topics can be re-cut through multiple pillars without
repeating yourself. `data/topics.json` is seeded with the first ~3 scripts
per pillar (29 total) so you have roughly a month of runway immediately.

1. **Behind the Curtain** — the real history behind the fiction (what you've
   been doing). Keep this as your anchor format, but it's only 1 of 10 angles.
2. **Villains You Root For** — opera/ballet's antiheroes vs. today's
   prestige-TV antiheroes (Sweeney Todd → Dexter, Phantom → Loki, Bluebeard →
   true crime). This taps directly into true-crime and "morally grey
   character" audiences who don't currently know they're your audience.
3. **Sound & Sample** — where classical/opera music physically shows up in
   trailers, hip-hop, and film scores. Highly shareable because you can pair
   it with actual audio clips people already recognize.
4. **Opening Night Chaos** — riots, flops, scandals, censorship fights. Pure
   drama, no "you have to like opera" barrier to entry.
5. **AI Meets the Classics** — direct expansion of your Coppelia angle:
   automatons, voice cloning, AI companions. This is your highest-leverage
   pillar for tech-audience crossover and trend-relevance (search interest in
   AI is not slowing down).
6. **Costume, Design & Fashion** — gothic aesthetics → horror movies, pointe
   shoes and the real physical cost. Good for fashion/aesthetic-TikTok
   crossover and "satisfying reveal" hooks.
7. **Hidden in Plain Sight** — classical/opera/ballet quietly embedded in
   things your audience already watches (Bridgerton, anime, video games).
   This is your best discovery-algorithm pillar — you're riding an existing
   show/game's search traffic.
8. **Composer Drama** — real-life flops, feuds, and fights with censors.
   Personality-driven, good for a recurring "history's messiest artists"
   sub-series.
9. **Myth to Modern** — myth → opera → modern film/Broadway (Orpheus →
   Hadestown, Faust → every "deal with the devil" movie). Great for tying to
   whatever mythology-adjacent movie/show is trending that month.
10. **Carmen's Hip-Hop Lineage** — your Carmen crossover, deepened: Carmen
    Jones (1954), why hip-hop opera keeps returning to Carmen specifically,
    and the reverse trade — pop stars borrowing ballet/opera visual language.
11. **Classical Music Hiding in Video Games (90s-2000s)** — real, verified
    classical/opera needle-drops and radio stations buried in games: GTA
    III's "Double Clef FM" (classical/opera, favorite station of the game's
    Mafia families), GTA IV's "The Journey" (contemporary classical
    including Philip Glass), Hitman: Blood Money's use of Schubert's "Ave
    Maria," and Katamari Damacy's orchestral tracks. One myth-busting entry
    is included too: GTA San Andreas does *not* have a classical station
    (people misremember CSR 103.9, which is actually new jack swing/R&B) —
    a good "sounding classy vs. actually classical" hook on its own.
12. **Pure History** — standalone historical deep-dives with no pop-culture
    bridge required: the 1661 founding of ballet as an institution, the
    invention of the tutu, the history of castrati singers, Clara Schumann's
    career being overshadowed by her husband's, the invention of the piano,
    Diaghilev's Ballets Russes, Maria Callas reviving bel canto, and why
    opera orchestras are hidden in a pit. This pillar exists specifically so
    not every script has to lean on a modern hook to justify itself.

**A correction worth knowing:** Tony Hawk's Pro Skater's soundtrack is punk,
ska, and underground hip-hop — there's no classical music in it. If that
franchise comes up as a "classical crossover" idea, the accurate angle is
that it pioneered the *licensed soundtrack* model in games (contrasted with
GTA/Hitman's classical licensing), not a hidden classical needle-drop.

## Where the real gaps are right now

- **You have an origin-story format but no recurring characters/series
  branding.** Give each pillar (or a rotating subset) a consistent on-screen
  title card and name — e.g. "Villains You Root For," "Hidden Opera" — so the
  algorithm and your audience learn to expect a series, not one-off facts.
- **No audio strategy yet.** Pillar 3 (Sound & Sample) is built specifically
  to fix this — pairing a fact with a recognizable audio clip (trailer music,
  a sampled riff) is one of the highest-performing hook types on TikTok
  because the sound itself stops the scroll before the words do.
- **No tech/AI-audience crossover yet**, despite Coppelia already proving the
  audience wants it. Pillar 5 leans into that on purpose — post those on days
  AI is in the news cycle for extra reach.
- **No use of trending/existing-fandom search traffic.** Pillar 7 rides shows
  people already search for (Bridgerton, anime, specific games) instead of
  requiring people to already be interested in opera to find you.
- **No posting cadence signal beyond "when I feel like it."** The automation
  below forces a daily minimum; treat weekly stretch goals (2-3 posts/day
  across formats — a fact video, a duet/stitch reacting to a trend, a
  behind-the-scenes) as the next step once daily posting is consistent.

## Hook formula being used across all 29 scripts

`[Surprising claim that sounds false] + [tiny proof point] + [why it matters now]`

e.g. "Sweeney Todd was a Victorian tabloid rumor before he was a musical —
and Tim Burton is the third person to steal him." Keep new scripts to this
shape: lead with the counterintuitive claim, not the topic name.

## Growth tactics beyond scripting

- **Cross-post identically to TikTok and Instagram Reels/YouTube Shorts** —
  same script, same hook, three algorithms. Don't create separate content per
  platform yet; volume and consistency matter more at this stage than
  platform-native tweaks.
- **Comment-bait CTAs** (already built into several scripts — "which one did
  you know?", "name a modern movie that's secretly a Faust story") drive
  replies, which the algorithm rewards more than likes.
- **Duet/stitch opportunities**: Pillar 7 topics (Bridgerton, anime, video
  games) are easy to stitch onto trending clips from those fandoms.
- **Batch filming**: since a script lands in your inbox every morning, film
  3-5 at once on your best-lighting day and schedule the rest, rather than
  filming same-day every day.
- **Track which pillar performs best after ~3-4 weeks** and skew the next
  batch of `topics.json` additions toward it — the JSON file is meant to be
  a living document, not a fixed list.
